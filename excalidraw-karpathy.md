# How Excalidraw Got Discovered Through Andrej Karpathy's Video

One month ago, there was a sudden spike in branded organic searches for Excalidraw, an online whiteboarding tool.

What happened that day? Andrej Karpathy had released his 3.5-hour deep learning video, which has now reached 1.7 million views.

The interesting part is that he never explicitly endorsed Excalidraw. He simply used it well throughout the presentation, which created a stronger impression than a sponsored promotion could have.

Within weeks, developers on my team were using it.

This made me think about product discovery in the AI era. Traditional organic channels are becoming less effective as answer engines increasingly dominate search.

Yet people still want to discover new products.

Two ideas that are often overlooked in go-to-market discussions for software startups are mimesis and signaling.

Through mimesis, we imitate other people and their desires. Through signaling, we show that we are like them. This loop can create significant value for a business.

Examples:

- Obsidian, Superhuman, and Notion grew when influencers showcased their workflows.
- Vercel and Next.js gained traction when respected developers shared their projects.
- VS Code and Figma setups spread through tech YouTube.

As builders, we should design distribution strategies around these human tendencies.
